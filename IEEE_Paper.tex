\begin{abstract}
Novice researchers often spend substantial time on literature discovery, gap identification, and hypothesis formulation before they can begin meaningful experimentation or analysis. Conventional large language model systems can accelerate writing and summarization, but they remain vulnerable to hallucination and knowledge staleness because their outputs are bounded by static training data. Traditional retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) improves grounding, yet it typically relies on fixed retrieval flows that are not well suited for the multi-hop reasoning demands of scientific exploration. This paper presents an agentic generative AI research co-pilot that transforms a high-level research topic into a structured, literature-grounded research blueprint through a seven-stage pipeline. The system combines LangGraph-based orchestration, real-time ArXiv retrieval, Facebook AI Similarity Search(FAISS)-based semantic search, hypothesis generation, mathematical formalization, roadmap construction, and critic-driven refinement within a unified architecture. The backend is implemented using FastAPI, SQLite with SQLAlchemy, JWT-based authentication, bcrypt password hashing, server-sent event streaming, Google Gemini 2.0 Flash, an in-memory FAISS index, and all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings. Across five research domains, the system produced complete blueprints with an average end-to-end latency of 85.58 seconds while retrieving 15 papers per query. The reported Research Utility Score was 82.6 percent, supported by 100 percent logical coherence, 100 percent formalization accuracy, 100 percent roadmap week completeness, 100 percent hypothesis relevance, 87.1 percent reasoning clarity, and 69.8 percent hypothesis novelty. These results indicate that an agentic, literature-grounded pipeline can offer novice researchers a practical scaffold for early-stage research design.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The rapid expansion of scholarly literature has made research initiation increasingly difficult, especially for students, novice investigators, and interdisciplinary researchers who must simultaneously understand prior work, identify gaps, and formulate testable ideas. In many cases, literature review and early-stage ideation consume weeks or months, creating a bottleneck between curiosity and rigorous research execution. This challenge is not merely a productivity issue; it also affects the quality and confidence of early research decisions.

Large language models have demonstrated strong capabilities in summarization, explanation, and text generation, making them attractive assistants for academic work. However, standard LLM-based assistants often generate plausible but unverifiable claims, and they struggle to remain current when fast-moving domains evolve beyond the model's training window. As a result, a student may receive fluent output without reliable literature grounding.

Retrieval-augmented generation partially addresses this limitation by grounding model outputs in external documents \cite{lewis2020rag}. Nevertheless, conventional RAG systems often follow static retrieve-then-generate patterns and are less effective when a task requires decomposition, iterative reasoning, self-evaluation, and synthesis across multiple technical sources. Scientific ideation is inherently multi-step: a broad topic must be decomposed, connected to recent literature, examined for unresolved questions, and translated into formal hypotheses.

To address this gap, this work proposes an agentic generative AI research co-pilot that operates as a coordinated pipeline of specialized stages rather than a single response engine. The system accepts a high-level research topic and produces a six-part research blueprint consisting of structured reasoning, literature support, semantic gap analysis, testable hypotheses, mathematical formalization, and a learning roadmap, with a critic stage providing iterative quality control.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item A LangGraph-based agentic architecture that transforms an unstructured research topic into a structured and literature-grounded research blueprint.
\item A real-time retrieval design that dynamically queries ArXiv and builds a temporary FAISS index for each request, reducing dependence on stale, precomputed corpora.
\item A seven-stage workflow that integrates reasoning, retrieval, synthesis, formalization, educational planning, and critic-based refinement in one end-to-end system.
\item An evaluation framework based on the Research Utility Score, along with latency measurements and ablation-style analysis across major output dimensions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
Recent work on LLM-based research assistance has explored summarization, extraction, ideation, and collaborative agent systems. Tools such as Elicit focus on helping users search, summarize, and structure scientific knowledge \cite{stiennon2022elicit}, while broader agentic systems such as The AI Scientist \cite{lu2024aiscientist} and Agent Laboratory \cite{lal2024agentlab} investigate automated or semi-automated scientific workflows. These systems demonstrate the promise of AI in research support, but many are designed either for general experimentation pipelines or for advanced users rather than for novice-oriented research scaffolding.

Retrieval-augmented generation has become a core strategy for grounding language models in external evidence \cite{lewis2020rag}. Follow-up paradigms such as Self-RAG \cite{asai2024selfrag}, Adaptive-RAG \cite{jeong2024adaptiverag}, and corrective retrieval methods have shown that retrieval quality improves when the system can reflect, adapt, or revise its evidence-gathering behavior. The present system aligns with this direction by constructing a fresh retrieval index from current ArXiv results for each query, thereby emphasizing temporal relevance and query-specific grounding.

Multi-agent orchestration frameworks such as MetaGPT \cite{hong2024metagpt} and AutoGen \cite{wu2023autogen} have shown that complex tasks can benefit from decomposition into specialized, cooperating roles. LangGraph extends this philosophy through explicit state management and graph-based orchestration, making it well suited for pipelines that must preserve intermediate outputs and support selective re-execution. This capability is particularly useful in research ideation, where a weak hypothesis stage should be correctable without recomputing the entire workflow.

Generative AI has also been discussed as a learning companion and research assistant in educational contexts, with both opportunities and risks receiving attention. Concerns such as over-reliance, weak source verification, and academic misuse remain important. Accordingly, the proposed system is positioned as a blueprint generator that supports human researchers rather than as a substitute for human judgment.

\section{System Architecture}
The proposed platform is implemented as a graph-driven research assistant service in which each stage consumes and enriches a shared state object. The architecture is intended to support modularity, traceability, and controlled refinement rather than single-pass text generation. This design allows each component to focus on a specific transformation while preserving intermediate artifacts for downstream use.

At the infrastructure level, the backend uses FastAPI to support high-concurrency and asynchronous operations. Session persistence is handled through SQLite with SQLAlchemy, where generated research blueprints are stored in a \texttt{ResearchSession} table. Authentication is implemented with bcrypt for password hashing and JWT for stateless security.

The platform also includes a live streaming mechanism that uses server-sent events and an \texttt{asyncio.Queue} to send progress updates to the client during long-running generation. To avoid blocking the event loop, the agentic pipeline is offloaded through asynchronous orchestration mechanisms. This improves responsiveness for interactive use, especially because the full pipeline requires more than a minute on average.

The language generation backbone is Google Gemini 2.0 Flash with strict JSON-constrained outputs, which helps maintain reliable formatting across stages. For semantic retrieval, the system uses an in-memory FAISS index on the CPU and the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embedding model, which produces 384-dimensional dense vectors. Together, these components provide the operational foundation for structured reasoning and grounded retrieval.

\section{Methodology}
The methodology is organized as a seven-stage pipeline managed through a LangGraph state graph. Each stage receives the current research state, performs a specialized transformation, and returns structured output for the next stage. This arrangement improves debuggability and makes the system easier to refine than a monolithic prompt-driven design.

\subsection{Stage 1: Structured Chain-of-Thought Reasoning}
The pipeline begins by converting a user's broad topic into a structured JSON representation containing a topic summary, hierarchical subtopics, key concepts, difficulty level, and a simplified explanation. This stage establishes a semantic frame for the rest of the pipeline and reduces ambiguity before retrieval begins. By explicitly decomposing the problem first, the system improves consistency across later stages.

\subsection{Stage 2: ArXiv-Based Knowledge Acquisition}
Next, the system uses the concepts extracted in Stage 1 to query the ArXiv API and retrieve the top 15 relevant papers for the input topic. For each paper, it collects metadata such as title, abstract, authors, categories, and timestamp. This stage ensures that downstream reasoning is anchored in recent and externally sourced scientific material instead of relying only on model priors.

\subsection{Stage 3: Agentic Retrieval-Augmented Generation}
The retrieved abstracts are segmented and encoded into dense vector representations using the sentence embedding model. These vectors are stored in a temporary FAISS index, and cosine similarity is used to identify the most relevant chunks for the user's query context. The selected evidence set becomes the focused context passed to the generative stages.

The retrieval mechanism can be described formally as follows:
\begin{equation}
e_i = f_{\theta}(d_i) \in \mathbb{R}^{384}
\label{eq:embedding}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
q = f_{\theta}(Q)
\label{eq:query}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{sim}(q, e_i) = \frac{q \cdot e_i}{\lVert q \rVert \lVert e_i \rVert}
\label{eq:cosine}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
C = \operatorname{TopK}\big(\operatorname{sim}(q, e_i)\big)
\label{eq:topk}
\end{equation}

Here, each document chunk $d_i$ is mapped to an embedding $e_i$, the query $Q$ is embedded as $q$, and the top-$K$ most similar chunks define the context set $C$ used for synthesis.

\subsection{Stage 4: Hypothesis Generation}
With literature-grounded context in place, the system analyzes semantic gaps and produces specific, testable research hypotheses. Rather than summarizing the retrieved papers alone, this stage attempts to identify underexplored connections, limitations, or open problems that can motivate new work. The intent is to generate hypotheses that are both novel and researchable.

\subsection{Stage 5: Mathematical Formalization}
The generated hypotheses are then translated into mathematical form, including objective functions and constraints expressed in KaTeX-compatible syntax. This gives the resulting blueprint a stronger scientific structure and helps researchers move from conceptual ideas to formal problem statements. It is particularly useful in engineering, optimization, and computational research settings.

A generic refinement relation used by the system's validation loop is represented as follows:
\begin{equation}
\text{Output}_{t+1} = f(\text{Output}_t, \text{Feedback})
\label{eq:refinement}
\end{equation}

This equation captures the critic-guided update process in which the current output is revised using targeted feedback.

\subsection{Stage 6: Educational Learning Roadmap}
Because the system is designed for novice researchers, it also generates a structured, week-by-week learning roadmap describing what the user should study to execute the proposed research direction. The roadmap includes progressive learning objectives and activity planning rather than only technical deliverables. This stage bridges the gap between idea generation and practical execution.

\subsection{Stage 7: Critic-Based Validation and Refinement}
Finally, a critic agent evaluates the outputs of the prior stages using a discrete score from 1 to 10 across key dimensions such as reasoning logic, hypothesis novelty, mathematical formalization accuracy, and roadmap feasibility. If any component falls below the acceptance threshold of 7.5, the system rejects the blueprint, generates corrective feedback, and retries refinement up to two times. This self-correction loop functions as an internal quality-control mechanism.

\section{Evaluation and Results}
The system was evaluated across five research domains: quantum finance, sodium-ion batteries, AI literature review, edge computing, and blockchain genomics. Each domain was tested using one high-level topic string, and three independent runs per topic were compared against a zero-shot Gemini baseline without retrieval. The experiments were intended as an initial multi-domain assessment rather than a final benchmark.

\subsection{Throughput and Latency}
The pipeline completed all tested runs without pipeline failure or state-graph exceptions. Each query retrieved 15 papers, and the average end-to-end latency across domains was 85.58 seconds. Domain-specific latency ranged from 74.75 seconds for AI literature review to 103.16 seconds for blockchain genomics.

\subsection{Quality Metrics}
The reported Research Utility Score was 82.6 percent. Sub-metrics included 100 percent logical coherence, 100 percent step validity, 100 percent objective correctness, 100 percent symbol consistency, 100 percent hypothesis relevance, 100 percent gap articulation, 100 percent week completeness, 87.1 percent reasoning clarity, and 69.8 percent hypothesis novelty. These values indicate that the system is strongest in structural consistency and formal correctness, while novelty remains the more variable dimension.

The composite Research Utility Score is defined as a weighted combination of six component scores:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{RUS} = {}&0.25\,S_{\mathrm{hyp}} + 0.20\,S_{\mathrm{reas}} + 0.15\,S_{\mathrm{int}} \\
&+ 0.15\,S_{\mathrm{gen}} + 0.15\,S_{\mathrm{form}} + 0.10\,S_{\mathrm{road}}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:rus}
\end{equation}

This formulation emphasizes hypothesis quality and reasoning strength while still accounting for interpretability, generation quality, formalization, and roadmap completeness.

\subsection{Ablation and Qualitative Findings}
The manuscript reports an ablation-style analysis indicating strong contributions from reasoning, formalization, interpretability, and roadmap generation, with lower scores in generation quality because diversity-oriented metrics penalized n-gram overlap. Reported module values included 100 for reasoning, 100 for formalization, 78.8 for hypothesis generation, 23.4 for generative quality, 95.7 for interpretability, and 100 for roadmap quality.

\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Conclusion}
The results suggest that agentic orchestration can improve the usefulness of AI-assisted research ideation by combining current literature retrieval, structured decomposition, semantic filtering, formal hypothesis construction, and self-correction within one workflow. The strongest outcomes appear in coherence, formalization accuracy, and roadmap completeness, which are especially important for novice users who need dependable structure at the start of a research project.

At the same time, the current evaluation remains limited in scope because it covers only five research areas and relies on an internally defined utility metric. The manuscript also notes that average latency of about 85 seconds is a practical bottleneck caused partly by sequential API calls, suggesting future optimization through parallel execution and partial local deployment. Broader benchmarking on datasets such as ArXiv-QA \cite{es2023ragas} and additional expert human evaluation would strengthen the evidence base.

In conclusion, this paper presents a coherent agentic generative AI research co-pilot for transforming broad research topics into literature-grounded, testable, and mathematically formalized blueprints. By integrating real-time ArXiv retrieval, dynamic FAISS-based RAG, LangGraph orchestration, and critic-based refinement, the system addresses key weaknesses of static LLM assistance, especially hallucination and stale knowledge. The reported performance indicates that such systems can serve as practical research accelerators for students and novice researchers when used as guided planning tools rather than autonomous scientific authors.
